# Literature survey: deep-learning surrogates predicting wall shear stress (WSS) from vessel geometry (2016–2026)

Compiled 2026-09-21. Scope: models that map arterial geometry (mesh / point cloud / graph / image-projection / centerline) to WSS-type fields (instantaneous WSS, TAWSS, OSI, RRT, ECAP) as CFD replacements. Velocity/pressure/FFR-only works are listed in a secondary table because they are the methodological parents of the WSS works. Every number below was read from the primary source (arXiv/journal full text, PMC full text, or Europe PMC/Crossref abstract) unless flagged "(unverified)".

Conventions used in the tables
- Input type: SURF = surface mesh with connectivity required (geodesics/mesh convolution); PC = point cloud / vertices only (connectivity not needed by the network; features may still be precomputed on a mesh); VOL = volume (tetrahedral) mesh; IMG = 2D projection / unwrapped grid / rendered views; CL = centerline; PARAM = scalar shape parameters.
- BC-in: whether inflow / boundary conditions are network inputs (Y), fixed across the dataset and therefore not an input (N), or unknown (?).
- Metric pooling: "per-case" = metric computed per geometry then mean/median over test geometries; "pooled" = computed over all points/pixels of the test set at once.

## C. Notes per coordinator questions

(a) Real cohorts vs synthetic. Only five WSS works train mainly on real anatomies: Rygiel 2025 AAA (100 + 118 real scans), Griffo 2026 (1078 real vessels), Gharleghi 2022 (101 real + 2626 synthetic, real test), Alamir 2024 (50 real + 550 derived), Morales Ferez 2021 (54 + 114 real). Everything from the Twente coronary line (Suk 2022/2024, LaB-GATr), Su 2020, Sarkhosh 2025, Luo 2026, Sheng 2026 ×2, Li 2025 (PoF), Lannelongue 2026, Garnier 2025, Faisal 2025 is trained on synthetic / idealised / SSM-generated geometries, with at most a handful of real cases used for qualitative or zero-shot tests. Reported cross-domain drops when moving synthetic → real: Suk 2024 (NMAE 0.5 % → 87.4 % on one CCTA case), Sheng CMPB 2026 (NMAE 2.59 % → 19.1 % zero-shot), Bafghi 2026 (rel-L2 0.30 → 0.56 patient-disjoint).

(b) Pooled vs per-case. Twente papers report per-artery ε / NMAE then mean / median / 75th percentile over the test set (NMAE denominator = max GT magnitude over the *whole test split*, which makes NMAE tiny relative to ε). Rygiel AAA: per-case mean ± std; NMAE = MAE / max_{p,t}‖τ_true‖. Faisal: pooled over image pixels, NMAE relative to the colour-map intensity maximum. Alamir: pooled over grid points, normalised by a fixed 33.33 Pa range. Luo 2026: pooled across all test scenarios. Griffo 2026: per-vessel median [IQR] of lesion-/vessel-averaged errors, plus Dice of high-WSS regions. Sheng 2026 (arXiv): rL2* uses per-case maximum. No paper in this set reports a pooled or per-case R² for WSS fields except Malek 2025 (parameter regression) and Luo 2026 (Pearson R 0.94 pooled); Li 2025 PoF gives r = 0.89.

(c) Learning-curve / data-size statements. Suk 2024 CBM: both GEM-GCN and PointNet++ "reach good accuracy with ca. 1000 training samples" (Fig 7). Suk FIMH 2023: 160 samples ≈ 1600 samples; 16 samples doubles PointNet++ ε (10.9 → 23.9 %) but SEGNN only 1.5× (9.3 → 14.1 %). Rygiel active learning: 64 physics-selected ≈ 128 random samples. Alamir 2024: loss keeps decreasing with training-set size (Fig S9); synthetic augmentation required because only 50 real cases. Rygiel AAA 2025: 90 real geometries × 4 BCs per fold suffice for external generalisation; feature ablation shows normals are the single biggest gain (0.745 → 0.392 Pa). Griffo 2026 (1078 vessels) and GReAT (8449-shape pre-training for 49 patients) are the two attempts at scale on real data; GReAT numbers not accessible.

(d) Robustness to sampling density / segmentation noise / no-volume-mesh deployment.
- Rygiel 2025 AAA: remeshing test set 1.25 → 1.0 / 1.5 mm — no statistically significant change (Wilcoxon p > 0.05); model consumes surface vertices only (geodesic features need the surface mesh but no volume mesh).
- Suk 2024 CBM Sec. 4.5: GEM-GCN "does overfit, to some extent, to mesh connectivity" — high vertex density is read as high WSS; PointNet++ more robust (max-aggregation).
- Suk 2025 deep vectorised operators: re-sampling query points non-significant for MLP / LaB-GATr / LaB-VaTr; PointNet++ sensitive (+0.021 approx. disp.).
- Bafghi 2026: inference cost invariant to surface query count; 20 % centerline noise raises error 0.280 → 0.531 (needs a centerline + radii only).
- You 2026 PoF: input spatial resolution matters for template-based WSSNet (numbers unverified).
- Deployment without a volume mesh: all SURF/PC/IMG/CL methods (rows 1–16, 20–25) need only the lumen surface (STL) or its point cloud; VOL methods (Lannelongue, Garnier, Suk FIMH) need a tetrahedral mesh. Only Rygiel 2025 explicitly tests segmentation-derived meshes of a different centre (AAA-L, different scanner/site).

(e) Inflow / BC as input. Y: Suk 2024 (conditional experiment only), Rygiel 2025, Gharleghi 2022, Alamir 2024, Sheng arXiv 2026, Li PoF 2025, Lannelongue, Garnier, Nannini benchmark, Bafghi, Deep vectorised operators. N (fixed BC baked into dataset): Suk 2022, LaB-GATr, Su 2020, Sarkhosh, Luo 2026, Sheng CMPB 2026, Morales Ferez, Faisal (unverified). ?: Griffo 2026, Kang 2024, You 2026, GReAT.

## D. State of the art in ten lines

1. Metric ranges (synthetic coronary, steady WSS, Twente convention): NMAE 0.4–1.6 % (denominator = test-set max WSS), approximation error ε 5.5–12 % (LaB-GATr 5.5 %, GEM-GCN 7.6–7.8 % single / 11–12 % bifurcation); with a "max−min range" or colour-map denominator other groups quote 0.36–6 %.
2. Real-patient coronary TAWSS (Griffo 2026, n = 1078): median vessel-averaged error 0.48 Pa / 23.6 %, high-WSS Dice 0.88, R 0.67 raw → 0.89 after per-vessel normalisation; Alamir 2024 on 10 real patients: NMAE 6.0 %, MAE 2.1 Pa.
3. Real-patient AAA transient WSS (Rygiel 2025, LaB-GATr): MAE 0.27–0.30 Pa, approximation disparity 0.51–0.57, cosine 0.66–0.68; TAWSS MAE 0.14–0.15 Pa; OSI MAE 0.05–0.06; robust to 1.0–1.5 mm remeshing, degrades at high inflow.
4. Intracranial aneurysms: synthetic-only training dominates (AneuG-Flow 14k steady + ~800 pulsatile); best max-normalised rL2 2.8 % for transient WSS but conventional rL2 27 %; OSI is consistently the hardest cycle-integrated quantity (rL2 68 %; Garnier: "lower accuracy"; Rygiel: largest inflow sensitivity).
5. No field-level R² is reported by the major groups; Pearson R 0.89–0.94 appears in Luo 2026 (pooled) and Li 2025; Dice/IoU of high-/low-WSS regions is reported only by Griffo 2026 (0.84–0.88) and Morales Ferez (TPR 70–77 %).
6. Dataset sizes: 1000–2000 synthetic geometries is the norm; real cohorts are 23–127 patients except Griffo (748 patients) and Rygiel AAA (129 patients / 218 scans); learning curves plateau around ~1000 synthetic samples (Suk 2024) and SE(3)-equivariance halves the data needed (FIMH 2023; 160 ≈ 1600).
7. Synthetic → real transfer is the open failure mode: NMAE 0.5 % → 87 % (Suk 2024, one case), 2.6 % → 19 % (Sheng 2026), rel-L2 0.30 → 0.56 (Bafghi 2026); only Rygiel 2025 and Griffo 2026 demonstrate external / multi-centre real generalisation.
8. Inputs: surface-mesh or point-cloud models with normals, curvature, geodesic distance to inlet and a scalar inflow feature are the strongest recipe (Rygiel Table 3: normals alone halve the error); volume-mesh autoregressive GNNs (Lannelongue, Garnier) give full velocity but are the only ones needing a tetrahedral mesh.
9. Inference is 35 ms – 15 s per geometry on a GPU (< 5 s typical for surface GNNs; ~1 min per cycle for volume rollouts) versus 10 min – 24 h CFD.
10. Nobody in this set reports per-case vs pooled R² with seed variance or a density/noise sweep of the input point cloud beyond a single remeshing experiment; robustness to segmentation noise remains untested outside Bafghi's centerline-noise sweep.
